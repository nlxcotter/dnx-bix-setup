# How a PM Gets Started — Onboarding Reference

> The reusable onboarding reference for any incoming project PM: the mission-first way to begin, so each new PM opens as smoothly as the last. Authored by the TCS PM (the 1st PM) 2026-07-16 at Cotter's direction, curated by the GM. **Classified PM-Teachable** (also feeds the `dnx-biz-setup` training program). Framing set by Cotter: lead with the mission and the understanding, NOT with pitfalls. No em-dashes, no periods in headings.

## The mission, the understanding, the open

**Start with the mission, not the task list.** Before any work, understand what the project actually IS and why it matters. For TCS it was never "build a website." It was: rebuild a real 20-year local business on DashNex Business and prove that a well-structured, AI-legible site lifts its search and AI visibility, as a repeatable AIRI case study. The moment you understand you are *proving something*, with a real person's livelihood as the subject, every decision has a north star. Find your project's real mission first. It changes how you weigh everything after.

**Understand where you sit.** The chain is **Cotter > GM > PM.** You (PM) own your project's build and submit up to the GM; the GM is the hub and reviewer; Cotter's direct word overrides anything. The comms board (`Systems/comms/<project>.md`) is the shared nervous system between you and the GM. Learn it before you write to it.

**Read in, in this order, before you touch anything:**
1. **The Canon** (`Systems/canon/`): governance (how we operate), working-with-cotter (how he thinks), gnat-standard (the quality bar). This is the single source of truth; point to it, never copy it, and re-read it on check-in.
2. **This onboarding reference** (`dnx-biz-setup/docs/onboarding-how-a-pm-begins.md`): how a PM begins.
3. **Your own directives** (`<project>/docs/directives.md`): your standing orders.
4. **The whole comms board**, start to finish. It is two-way, so reading it is half the job.
5. **The project's own record**: the "before" baseline, the living build doc, and the code. Know the goal and the current state before you propose anything.

**Understand the owner, because you work WITH him, not just for him.** Cotter wants **real over nice**, so correct him the moment he is wrong. He is **visual-first**, so lead with the rendered thing, not a wall of text. He has a specific voice and specific standards (plain customer copy at a 6th-grade level, no em-dashes, difficulty-based pricing, honest specifics over hype). Match them early and the trust compounds fast.

**How it opened, what made the 1st PM productive quickly:**
- Read before acting, and reconcile the board and the code before reporting anything.
- Anchor every piece of work to the mission, so you are building toward the proof, not just clearing a list.
- Match his voice and standards from the start, so the work feels like his, not yours.
- Close loops cleanly (edit the original marker, keep the living docs current) so nothing drifts.
- When you do not know something about him or the business, ask or interview him. Never invent him to fill a gap.

**The mission, restated, carry this:** you are not shipping tasks, you are proving something real for a real person. Understand that first, hold the owner's standards as your own, keep the shared record honest and current, and the rest follows.
